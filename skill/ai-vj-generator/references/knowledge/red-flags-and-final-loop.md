# Red flags, quality standard, final loop, quick reference

> The V3 stop-and-correct list and the order of priorities (§55–§59). Still binding; `../quality-gates.md` turns it into a score.

---

# 55. RED FLAGS

Stop and correct if:

- you start from a shader
- you start from a favorite generator
- five concepts are parameter variations
- the same visual grammar dominates every concept
- color is the main source of variation
- motion is only rotation
- motion is only noise displacement
- everything is centered
- everything is full-frame
- everything glows
- every concept uses particles
- every concept uses noise
- references are copied instead of analyzed
- an artist's signature style is being imitated
- a reference is mentioned without extracting a principle
- theory is mentioned without influencing a parameter
- technical sophistication is replacing artistic intention
- the animation looks like a preset
- the artifact renders but does not communicate the briefing
- audio affects everything
- the canvas ignores the target surface
- unsupported transport is claimed
- uncontrolled randomness is used
- a new renderer is created
- recent visual strategies are being repeated
- a previous project, an example file, a stock palette or the STANDARD stack is the starting point
- the composition list is the same five generic names (structure, flow, density, rhythm, transformation)
- attachments were not analyzed, or were analyzed only by eye with no measurement when the scripts were available
- an attachment is reproduced, traced or embedded instead of becoming a principle
- a composition is reported that was never opened and looked at
- text, a face or a logo crosses the gutter of a fold
- a cut-out is expected to work across layers (it only works inside its own layer; in white-alpha use the 43% field)
- a code layer uses `Math.random`, a clock or kept state, or a logo/font was invented instead of supplied by the user
- hairlines, scanlines or small HUD on a wall: glitch is blocks, elements are large

# 56. CREATIVE QUALITY STANDARD

The final animation should feel like:

> THE CONCEPT CREATED THE ANIMATION.

Not:

> THE SHADER WAS GIVEN A CONCEPT.

Prioritize:

```text
INTENTION
over
EFFECT

CONCEPT
over
TECHNIQUE

MEANING
over
DECORATION

COMPOSITION
over
COMPLEXITY

MOTION
over
RANDOMNESS

SYSTEM
over
PRESET

AUTHORSHIP
over
AESTHETIC NOISE

DIVERSITY
over
SAFE REPETITION
```

For every substantial generation:

```text
UNDERSTAND
↓
ANALYZE REFERENCES
↓
INTERPRET
↓
RESEARCH FROM KNOWLEDGE
↓
CONCEPTUALIZE
↓
DEFINE SEMIOTIC INTENT
↓
DESIGN ART DIRECTION
↓
DEFINE ANIMATION PRINCIPLE
↓
DEFINE VISUAL GRAMMAR
↓
SELECT TECHNIQUE
↓
DESIGN GENERATIVE SYSTEM
↓
BUILD
↓
OPEN ARTIFACT
↓
OBSERVE
↓
CRITIQUE
↓
MUTATE
↓
VALIDATE
↓
DELIVER
```

The renderer is the execution layer.

The Creative Knowledge System is the theoretical foundation.

The Skill is the creative intelligence.

The briefing is the source of intent.

The technique is the material.

The artifact is the final visual argument.
---

# 58. FINAL RULE

When uncertain between two solutions, choose the one that:

1. communicates the concept more clearly;
2. creates stronger visual hierarchy;
3. has more intentional motion;
4. uses the reference as principle rather than imitation;
5. introduces meaningful visual diversity;
6. is technically appropriate for the target;
7. feels authored rather than preset-driven.

The objective is not to maximize effects.

The objective is to create an original audiovisual system with conceptual, aesthetic, temporal and technical coherence.
